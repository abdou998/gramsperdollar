Cooking for one is awkward in a specific way. Chicken comes in 3 and 5 lb bags, recipes
serve four or six, and by Thursday you're eating the same thing for the fifth time or
throwing half of it out.

This plan is built around those problems. It covers breakfast, lunch and dinner for four
days, gives you about **{plan.per_day.protein_g} g of protein a day**, and costs about
**{usd(st.cost_per_day)} a day** in ingredients. Two of the meals are made in one prep session;
breakfast takes five minutes each morning.

Every recipe below shows its protein per serving and what it costs, plus the yellow tag:
**grams of protein you get for each dollar**. Higher is better value.

## The prep session

Plan on roughly an hour and a half. The trick is running the one-pot dinner and the
lunch at the same time, so nothing sits waiting.

1. **The night before:** move the chicken from the freezer to the fridge so it's thawed.
2. **Start the dinner pot.** Brown the chicken pieces, then the onion and spices, then add
   the rice, lentils, tomatoes and water. Once it's simmering with the lid on, leave it.
3. **While it simmers, marinate the lunch chicken.** Slice the breasts into thin cutlets
   and coat them in the lemon, garlic and rosemary mix.
4. **Steam the green beans and carrots,** then rinse them under cold water and drain.
5. **Finish the pot.** Stir in the frozen peas, put the lid back on and take it off the heat.
6. **Grill the lunch chicken** in batches, then the squash in the same pan.
7. **Portion everything** into 8 containers: 4 dinners, 4 lunches. Keep the bell pepper
   slices in a separate small box so they stay crisp.
8. **Optional:** measure the dry pancake ingredients (whey, baking powder, cinnamon) into
   4 jars. Each morning, add an egg and milk and you're cooking in a minute.

Cooked chicken and rice dishes keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge, so this plan stops at four days.
If you want a fifth day, freeze one dinner portion on prep day and thaw it overnight.

## Where the money goes

Two things stand out in the numbers.

**The dinner is the best value on the page.** It has about the same protein per serving as
the grilled chicken lunch at roughly half the cost. Red lentils do a lot of that work:
they cook in the same time as white rice and add protein for very little money.

**The pancake's cost is almost all whey.** The egg, milk and banana add pennies; the scoop
of isolate is most of the price. If you want breakfast cheaper, a less expensive whey
concentrate is the biggest lever, at the cost of a few grams of protein.

## How the numbers are worked out

Protein and calories come from USDA FoodData Central, except the whey, which uses the
product's own label. Costs use Walmart.com prices for Sacramento, CA, checked October 2026,
and count only the amount each recipe uses, not the whole pack. Prices in your area will
be different, so treat them as a guide to which meals are good value rather than an exact bill.
