If you shop in Houston and want the most protein for your money, the short version is:
**Aldi is cheapest food by food, H-E-B is cheapest for a whole week of meals, and Walmart
rarely wins either.**

We priced the same {t.rows.length} protein staples at one Aldi, one H-E-B and one Walmart
in Houston in October 2026, then worked out how many grams of protein each dollar buys.
Here's what came out.

## The short answer

- **Aldi** gives the most protein per dollar on **{wins('aldi-tx')} of {t.rows.length} foods**:
  chicken breast, tofu, dry and canned beans, peanut butter, oats, Greek yogurt, ground turkey
  and more.
- **H-E-B** wins **{wins('heb-tx')}**: red lentils, whey protein and frozen shrimp. More usefully,
  it's the only one of the three where you can buy our whole 4-day meal plan in one trip at the
  lowest cost: **{usd(heb.cost_per_day)} a day**.
- **Walmart** wins **{wins('walmart-tx')}** (canned chicken, by a tenth of a gram), with
  {t.ties} tie. Its 4-day plan costs {usd(wmt.cost_per_day)} a day, about
  {pct(wmt.cost_per_day, heb.cost_per_day)}% more than H-E-B.
- **Willing to make two stops?** Aldi for the staples, H-E-B for lentils and protein powder.

## Where the difference is big

**Chicken breast.** Aldi's fresh family pack (${g('aldi-tx', 'chicken breast').price}/lb) and
H-E-B's fresh value pack ($2.23/lb) are almost level at {gpd('aldi-tx', 'chicken breast')} and
{gpd('heb-tx', 'chicken breast')} g per $1. Walmart's store-brand breasts at this store come as
a frozen 5 lb bag and give {gpd('walmart-tx', 'chicken breast')} g per $1, roughly a quarter
less. Chicken is the backbone of most meal prep, so this one matters more than anything else
on the list.

**Tofu.** Aldi's extra-firm tofu gives {gpd('aldi-tx', 'tofu firm')} g per $1, about double
Walmart's {gpd('walmart-tx', 'tofu firm')} (Walmart has no store-brand tofu here, so it's a
name brand). H-E-B sits in the middle at {gpd('heb-tx', 'tofu firm')}.

**Ground turkey 93/7.** Aldi and H-E-B both charge $4.89, but Aldi's pack is 19.2 oz and
H-E-B's is 16 oz. Same price, 20% more turkey.

**Peanut butter and dry black beans.** Aldi and Walmart sell the big packs (40 oz jar, 2 lb bag);
H-E-B's store brand is in smaller packs, which cost more per gram. If H-E-B is your store,
look for the bigger size.

## Where it's basically a tie

Eggs and cottage cheese are level at all three, less than 1% apart. Whole milk and pork loin
are level at Aldi and Walmart, with H-E-B about 15% behind. Don't drive across town for any of them.

One thing we didn't expect: **H-E-B and Walmart charged exactly the same, to the cent**, for
their store-brand eggs ($1.67), oats ($4.18 for 42 oz), Greek yogurt ($3.28), cottage cheese
($2.87), canned tuna ($0.96) and canned black beans ($0.92). Aldi was a little lower on all six.

## What H-E-B does better

- **Red lentils.** Aldi doesn't sell dry red lentils at this store, and H-E-B's
  ({gpd('heb-tx', 'red lentils')} g per $1) beat Walmart's ({gpd('walmart-tx', 'red lentils')}).
  At H-E-B they're the second-best protein value after dry black beans.
- **Whey protein.** Both H-E-B and Walmart sell the same isolate; per gram, H-E-B's tub works
  out cheaper. Aldi only sells a whey *blend*, which isn't the same product, so we left it out
  of the ranking.
- **Shrimp.** H-E-B's 2 lb bag edges out the smaller bags at the other two. But shrimp is the
  worst protein value of all {t.rows.length} foods at every store (about 7 g per $1, against
  60 to 70+ for dry beans), so buy it because you like it, not to save money.

## So which store should you use?

- **Only one store, the whole list:** H-E-B. Cheapest full week in our numbers, and it sells
  everything our recipes need.
- **Cheapest staples:** Aldi, especially for chicken breast, tofu, beans, peanut butter, oats
  and Greek yogurt.
- **Two stops:** Aldi for the staples, H-E-B for red lentils and protein powder.
- **Walmart:** fine if it's the one near you. It's about {pct(wmt.cost_per_day, heb.cost_per_day)}%
  dearer than H-E-B on our plan, but it's level on eggs, milk, cottage cheese and canned chicken. Just skip the
  frozen chicken breast bag if you can get fresh elsewhere.

## How we checked the prices

- One store per chain, all in Houston, checked October 2026.
- Regular prices only. Where a store had a sale running, we used the normal price.
- Each store's own everyday brand. Where there are two, the cheaper one per unit. Where a store
  has no store brand, a name brand.
- Meat sold by weight uses the per-pound price.
- **Aldi's online prices come through Instacart** and can be slightly different from the shelf.
  H-E-B's are its own in-store prices for that branch; curbside pickup costs a little more.
- Protein comes from USDA FoodData Central, except the whey, which uses the product's label.

Prices change, so treat these as a guide to which store is better value, not an exact bill.
We'll refresh them every few months, and add more Houston stores (Kroger and Target are next).
